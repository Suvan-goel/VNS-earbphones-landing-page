CYMBA / EARBUD COGNITION DEVICE — v0.3 CONCEPT MODEL
Third visual revision: requirements, references, dimensions and assumptions
21 October 2026

SOURCE HIERARCHY AND MODEL STATUS
Functional source: "Earbud Cognition Device: Technical Specification v0.3",
draft 18 October 2026, seven pages. Page references below refer to that PDF.
Appearance sources: the website project's assets/cymba-locked-design-reference.png
and active src/assets/earbuds/hero-premium-pair-v4.webp. The older unmounted
TechnologyTour imagery is not this revision's appearance target.

The PDF governs architecture, functions and contact separation. The images guide
silhouette, finishes and presentation. They do not supply dimensions or establish
anatomical fit. Where their styling conflicts with the PDF, the model keeps the
specified function; notably, the hub has one stop control rather than the
reference image's multiple buttons. CHANGES-TO-MATCH-IMAGERY.txt records this.

The model is a detailed, separable concept assembly. Shells, connectors, packages,
internal arrangements, visual materials and all dimensions not explicitly in the
PDF are proposed interpretations. Appearance is not evidence of electrical
function, manufacturability, human fit or medical performance.

SOURCE REQUIREMENTS REPRESENTED

1. Bilateral wired architecture (p.3)
Two mirrored earbuds connect through individual branches and a Y-split to a
clip-on hub. Left is the default stimulation side; right defaults to sensing.
BOTH earbuds contain stimulation and sensing hardware so software can select
the stimulation ear. Earbud contents include contacts, local sensors and audio.
The main processor, EEG amplification, stimulation generation, battery and radio
reserve are allocated to the hub. USB-C is the specified app connection.

2. Independent skin contacts (pp.3-5)
Each bud includes a fin-mounted cymba-conchae active contact, a separate
cavum-conchae stimulation return, TWO isolated canal EEG signal contacts and a
separate concha EEG reference. Stimulation and EEG contacts remain independent.
The right tragus-facing pad represents the shared DRL. The matching inactive
left pad is a disclosed interpretation of "identical hardware"; the PDF names
an active DRL only on the right.

The active contact now follows the rounded horn's actual tissue-facing curvature.
Its exposed curved CAD surface targets approximately 75.4 mm2 for every fin size,
within the specified 50-100 mm2 range. This replaces the previous planar ellipse;
it is not an 8 × 12 mm flat disc. The measured exposed area excludes backing and
edge faces. Each body return retains a 100 mm2 planar face, corresponding to
approximately 1 cm2. Real effective skin contact depends on fit and compression
and has not been established by either geometric area.

The active pad uses the permitted black conductive-silicone interpretation.
The body return uses a subdued warm Ag/AgCl-style appearance, while EEG parts
represent dry conductive polymer. These are material/finish choices, not tested
material specifications. The horn supports the curved active pad, and contact
inserts are flush or near flush where practical. Active impedance below 5 kOhm
at 1 kHz and dry EEG impedance below 50 kOhm, with a 20 kOhm target, remain source
requirements; the model does not demonstrate those values.

3. Ear tips and retention fins (pp.1-2, 6)
The fit kit contains five removable tip sizes and three retention-fin sizes.
Every tip has two curved 38-degree EEG sectors separated by two 142-degree
insulating gaps. The number of independent EEG signals follows the PDF; the
sector angles, placement and construction are design assumptions. Each sector
is a separate part following the tip's outer contour.

The tips have a smooth smoke-grey bell profile, rounded mouth, central acoustic
opening and a longer exposed neck matching the chosen imagery. The nozzle has
an open bore, retention feature and outlet mesh. Each curled fin includes its
separate curved active contact. Fin attachment, electrical interconnects,
retention force, contact pressure, insertion depth and anatomical coverage
remain design work. The fin grading is not a clinical ear-size classification.

4. PPG, motion and audio (p.5)
Each bud includes separate IR-emitter, green-emitter and receiver windows with
opaque optical separation, an IMU package and a dynamic speaker assembly.
The nominal IR PPG requirement is 256 Hz; the six-axis IMUs require 100 Hz.
These are functions assigned to representative components, not demonstrated
operation. A third IMU, shared clock, audio-driver allocation and EEG/DRL blocks
are represented in the hub. Package sizes and acoustic/optical design remain
provisional; no sensor accuracy or optical performance is inferred from shape.

5. Cable architecture (pp.3-4)
The exposed branch centrelines target 150 mm each; the exposed trunk targets
1000 mm. Internal termination depths are excluded. Generated reports record
actual modeled lengths. The trunk is coiled for presentation, not shown as a
prescribed wearing route. Its 3.0 mm outer diameter and the 2.1 mm branch diameter
are provisional choices, not dimensions supplied by the PDF.

The separate branch specimen contains 12 illustrative conductors: 2 stimulation,
4 EEG/reference/DRL and 6 digital sensing/audio/power/ground. The stimulation
pair is twisted in a foil-shield group; EEG has its own driven-shield group;
sensor/audio lines form a third group. The specimen explains bundle separation,
not final wire gauges or a connector pinout. It does not provide full conductor
routing or engineered shielding terminations.

The hub end has a keyed magnetic receiver and mating plug, with 24 illustrative
positions allocated 12 per ear. Magnet choice, polarity, contact travel, pinout,
release force and electrical ratings remain unresolved. Cable braid is a visual
finish assumption: the viewer generates a fine pattern following sampled CAD
centrelines. Individual textile fibres are not separate STEP solids, and their
appearance is not a claim of a specified braid construction.

6. Hub enclosure and controls (pp.3-5)
The slender hub contains hollow chassis and lid, seals, rear-access fasteners,
a rear spring clip, ONE physical stop, one status lens, USB-C and the keyed
breakaway interface. Its front is plain and unbranded. There are no added
volume or call controls from the appearance reference.

Internals include a battery allocation, main logic/EEG board, stimulation-domain
board, representative component envelopes, shielding allocation and a haptic
actuator. The stop actuator is a mechanical representation; an independently
operating stop circuit has not been designed or tested. The status indication
must not distinguish active from sham. The haptic placement is a design
interpretation for the specified gentle session-start/end feedback.

To obtain the revised slender form, provisional hub allocations were mapped
from the earlier layout by X × 25/34, Y × 77/64 and Z × 12/15. They are spatial
allocations, not real components whose availability has been verified. USB-C
geometry and contact pitch retain their original dimensions and are relocated
to the new port. The breakaway cable boot retains a circular cross section;
its length is adjusted. Per-part metadata records the final allocated envelope
before quoting any older construction-reference dimensions. Use those final
envelopes and the generated hub metrics, not the quoted older dimensions.

SELECTED PROVISIONAL DIMENSIONS — MILLIMETRES
The PDF supplies contact-area requirements and approximate cable lengths, but
no dimensional drawings for shells, fins, tips, boards, battery or connectors.
All dimensions below are therefore the modeled interpretation. Nominal design
values can differ slightly from interpolated-surface bounding boxes.

Earbud nominal body, excluding fin/tip/boot: 23.6 × 28.8 × 14.1.
Housing wall allocation: approximately 1.2, locally sculpted for parts/seats.
External nozzle inclination: -15 degrees about local Y; OD 6.3; bore 4.1.
The internal driver retains a +15-degree package axis and feeds the shared
acoustic chamber; acoustic response and chamber geometry remain unvalidated.
Tip nominal diameters XS/S/M/L/XL: 12.0 / 13.2 / 14.4 / 15.6 / 16.8.
Tip mouth nominal bore: 4.2; spline profile and wall thickness are provisional.
The bell profile is displaced 3.1 along the nozzle relative to its construction
origin to expose more neck; this is not a validated insertion-depth allowance.
Fin S/M/L profile factors: 0.94 / 1.00 / 1.10; active area about 75.4 mm2 each.
Speaker basket: 8.8 diameter; local sensor PCB allocation: 15 × 20 × 0.7.
Branch cable OD: 2.1; trunk OD: 3.0; Y-split body: 8 × 15 × 5.

Hub shell nominal X/Y/Z: 25 × 77 × 12.
Nominal side-wall allocations in X/Y: about 1.03 / 1.68; floor/roof: 1.04.
Recessed stop oval X/Y: about 7.06 × 11.55; edge about 0.056 below front face.
Rear clip: about 9.56 wide and 50.5 long; 4.0 rear extension and 3.08 free jaw
allocation. Spring material, load, travel and fatigue life remain unspecified.
Battery allocation X/Y/Z: about 16.91 × 40.91 × 3.52; capacity unspecified.
Main PCB base allocation X/Y/Z: about 20.59 × 60.16 × 0.64, with domain cutout.
Stimulation PCB allocation X/Y/Z: about 8.38 × 19.25 × 0.64.
Nominal lateral board-domain gap: about 2.43. This is a packaging allocation,
NOT a verified electrical clearance, isolation design or certification.
USB-C shell allocation X/Y/Z: 9.2 × 6.7 × 3.2; no manufacturer part selected.
Breakaway receiver face X/Z: about 19.12 × 5.60.
Breakaway plug body allocation X/Y/Z: about 18.38 × 5.78 × 5.12.
Breakaway boot circular root/exit radii: 2.50 / 1.65; length about 7.22.

Optical spacing, package sizes, screw construction, port tolerances, material
properties, transparency and surface finish are also provisional. The finish
shaders provide a visual interpretation, not measured optical/material data.

SOURCE AMBIGUITIES PRESERVED
- p.1 gives 60 minutes stimulation per session and 120 minutes per day; p.2
  describes a conflicting 180-minute per-session cap. The model selects no
  dosing policy and does not resolve this inconsistency.
- p.3 places a radio in the hub while specifying USB-C-only app connection.
  A package/antenna reserve is shown without assuming wireless app operation
  or supplying a tuned antenna.
- Identical hardware and the right-only active DRL do not fully define the
  left implementation. The inactive matching left pad remains disclosed.
- The Nurosym reference discusses a tragus clip. This product specifies active
  stimulation at its cymba-conchae fin, which is the site modeled here.
- The architecture summary's "only electrodes and sensors" is read alongside
  the explicit speaker requirement in the component table and p.5.

EDITABILITY AND VERIFICATION BOUNDARIES
editable.FCStd contains named, separately selectable solid components with
role/source/assumption metadata. It is not a fully constrained parametric
feature-history model or manufacturing release. The source scripts regenerate
the geometry; STEP supplies portable solid geometry; GLB supplies presentation
meshes with names and metadata. CAD units are millimetres and glTF units metres.
The Product view poses the pair independently for presentation; the complete
system restores their source arrangement. Posing does not change part geometry.
The assembled Product view also shows roughly 22 mm of each branch as a clipped
presentation lead, derived from its existing sampled centreline. These meshes
are excluded from the component registry, counts and CAD exports, hidden during
explosion, and replaced by the full source cables in the complete-system view.

model-manifest.json and validation.json record model content and generated
geometry results. CAD-INTERFERENCE-AUDIT.txt records the stated earbud/fit-kit
checks and any residual conceptual overlaps; HUB-VALIDATION.txt and
CABLE-VALIDATION.txt have their own stated scopes. Consult the current reports
for actual outcomes. A valid solid establishes only its geometric
representation, not tolerance, assembly motion, electrical integrity or fit.

No final circuit/PCB routing, selected bill of materials, electrode production
process, qualified material, biocompatibility result, verified current path,
electrical isolation, shielding performance, thermal assessment, acoustic test,
human fit study or ingress test is supplied. IP57 remains a source design
target. Impedance, sensor accuracy, retention force and all other performance
requirements remain unverified.

PORTABLE CABLE DISPLAY
The three GLB cable meshes are capped circular sweeps of their saved CAD
centreline samples, with 32 segments around each cross section. This avoids
a display triangulation sliver in the long swept CAD surface. CAD/STEP solids,
centreline lengths and metadata are unchanged. See CABLE-MESH-VALIDATION.txt.
